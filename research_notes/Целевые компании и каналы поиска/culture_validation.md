## 1. Results orientation: what shows outcomes over activity?

### Takeaway
The strongest public evidence of results orientation is a company that names a few outcome metrics, reports them every quarter, and explains trade-offs in its own words when they go against it (for example "operating income fell because we chose to invest"). Evidence of how a company behaves under stress (cuts, misses, policy reversals) counts for more than its values statements. At team level, results orientation can only be confirmed by asking for specific decisions, the metrics that moved, and who was accountable.

### Cited Findings
- MercadoLibre Q2 2026 (published 2026-08-05): net revenue and financial income $10.17B, +50% YoY; income from operations $683M (6.7% margin); operating income **down 17% YoY**, which the shareholder letter attributes to a deliberate choice of long-term value over near-term margin. On the call, a Goldman Sachs analyst pressed management on gross-margin compression that was larger than expected. ### Inferences
- HYPOTHESIS: signals for results orientation.
  - **Positive:** a short list of named outcome KPIs reported every quarter with the definitions unchanged (Nu: efficiency ratio, ARPAC, activity rate, cost to serve; MELI: GMV, TPV, operating margin). Trade-offs explained in plain language ("margin down because we invested in X"). OKRs or goals written as outcomes such as "reduce cost per active customer by Y" rather than "launch feature Z". Performance reviews that reference impact evidence. Post-mortems on failures that are published or at least accessible internally. Cuts that are targeted and explained, with internal redeployment.
  - **Red flags:** KPI definitions that change from one quarter to the next. "Launched / shipped / delivered" as the only success language. Reviews that reward visibility or hours. Interviewers who cannot name a metric their team moved in the last two quarters. Layoffs announced as "AI efficiency" with no explanation of what changed in the work. Repeated reorganizations with no stated outcome thesis.
- HYPOTHESIS: a company-level results orientation, visible in investor materials, does not transfer automatically to the specific team. Investor KPIs are company-level. An analytics leader must confirm which of them their own unit owns or influences. If it owns or influences none, the role is probably a cost-center or reporting role.
- HYPOTHESIS: both demo companies explicitly guide investors on efficiency and margin trade-offs (Nu's ~20% efficiency guide; MELI's "investing for long term" framing). That points to finance-led outcome discipline at the top. It says nothing about how a mid-level team's OKRs are set.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain internal OKR or goal-setting documentation for either company. No public 2025–2026 description of Nubank's or MELI's performance-review mechanics was found, except MELI's use of AI to summarize 360° feedback (see section 2).
- The full text of the MELI Q2 2026 shareholder letter, including whether Szarfsztejn signed it, was not read.

## 2. Meritocracy: signals of fair promotion and pay

### Takeaway
Fully public evidence of meritocracy is thin at any company. The usable signals are: a published or reconstructable leveling ladder (levels.fyi), how widely equity is granted, the company's own pay-equity and promotion-gap claims (self-reported), patterns in reviews (politics and favoritism complaints), and how the company treats dissent. Each signal is weak alone. Backchannel conversations about recent promotion and calibration cases remain the decisive evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Nu 20-F FY2025: "approximately 88% of our active employees have been granted share-based incentive awards as of December 31, 2025". The filing also describes a "culture of ownership" in which many employees hold a financial stake, and a workforce of more than 50 nationalities. VERIFIED_OFFICIAL: [Nu 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691493/000129281426002166/nuform20f_2025.htm)
- Nu governance: founder and CEO David Vélez owns 88.3% of Class B shares, about 74.4% of the voting power, and the filing flags this as a risk to minority shareholders. VERIFIED_OFFICIAL via summary: [StockTitan 20-F summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/NU/20-f-nu-holdings-ltd-files-annual-report-foreign-issuer-c6bf7bb401dd.html)
- levels.fyi, Nubank Software Engineering Manager, Brazil (page updated 2026-10-15; 37 submissions):
  - Manager: TC ≈ R$426K (base R$350K, stock R$71.5K, bonus R$4.7K).
  - Senior Manager: TC ≈ R$875K (base R$595K, stock R$276K).
  - Director and Senior Director levels are listed with no data. Median R$431K. RSUs vest 1/3 per year over 3 years.

### Inferences
- HYPOTHESIS: signals for meritocracy.
  - **Positive:** a visible ladder with consistent titles across functions, including business and analytics, not only engineering. Calibration committees with written promotion criteria. Broad equity (Nu's 88% is a strong ownership signal). Internal promotions into director and VP roles. Leaders with varied backgrounds, not one alma mater or one founder network. Disclosed pay-equity analysis. Reviews describing promotions tied to documented impact.
  - **Red flags:** repeated "politics", "panelinha", "QI (quem indica)" or "favoritism" in reviews from the same function. Promotions that depend on a single sponsor. Senior hires parachuted over internal candidates without explanation. Opaque level mapping at offer stage. Dissent punished without a transparent process.
- HYPOTHESIS: the November 2025 Nubank episode is ambiguous evidence. The company's account (ethics violations, sabotage) and the union's (punishment for criticism) conflict. A candidate should treat it as a TO_VERIFY topic for backchannel conversations: "Can people disagree with leadership in writing without career cost?" It is not proof in either direction.
- HYPOTHESIS: founder voting control (Vélez ~74% votes) is not a meritocracy flaw in itself. It raises the stakes of the question "how are disagreements with the founder or C-level resolved?" for anyone in a role close to decision-makers.
- HYPOTHESIS: levels.fyi in Brazil is engineering-heavy. Ladders for business, analytics and product roles are under-sampled, so a missing level (Director: "no data") means missing data, not a missing level.
- HYPOTHESIS: MELI's "no representative gap in promotions" is a company-run, unaudited claim. Treat it as an employer-branding statement until a backchannel source confirms it.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 public data on internal promotion rates at either company.
- I could not verify whether Nubank appears in the LinkedIn Top Companies Brasil 2025 list, or what either company scored in the 2026 edition, which I did not find.
- I could not confirm the outcome of the union meeting scheduled for 2025-11-19, or whether the July 2026 hybrid start went ahead as planned. No post-December-2025 source was found.
- The MELI LTRP mechanics (vesting tied to performance or to tenure) were not extracted.

## 3. Competence, including business, product and leadership talent

Competence is assessed through people evidence: the CXO bench and where its members came from, where talent goes when it leaves, tenure patterns, hiring-bar evidence, and the quality of public thinking (letters, talks, blogs). Public sources cover the top layer well. The business-side middle layer that an analytics leader would partner with is visible mainly through LinkedIn sampling and backchannel conversations.

### Inferences
- HYPOTHESIS: signals for competence.
  - **Positive:** a C-suite and their direct reports with track records at strong operators (Nu's CTO has 15 years at Amazon, about 7 at Google and an SVP role at Snap). Internal successors groomed over years (MELI's CEO joined in 2017 and rotated through strategy, logistics and marketplace, which is a strong internal-bench signal). Alumni who go on to found companies or take senior roles at strong firms. Public writing with real methods and numbers. Business-side leaders (product, marketing, credit, ops) who can talk about unit economics in interviews. A structured interview loop with a business case.
  - **Red flags:** heavy C-level churn without explanation. Business functions staffed mainly by long-tenured generalists with no external benchmark. Median tenure far below peers in the target function. Alumni not wanted by strong firms. Interviewers who cannot explain how their own unit makes money.
- HYPOTHESIS: MELI's growth was operations- and logistics-heavy (tech and product +11% while total headcount grew about 47%). Its business organization therefore has a large operational layer. For an analytics leader, "business side" partners may be operations managers more often than P&L product owners, so check which one the role pairs with.
- HYPOTHESIS: LinkedIn Talent Insights (Premium or Recruiter) can show headcount growth by function, median tenure and talent flows ("where alumni went"). It is the most practical scalable tool for this criterion, but it relies on self-reported profiles and lags reorganizations by months. Sample 20–30 profiles of directors in the target business unit by hand: previous employers, tenure, and promotions inside the company.

## 4. Data-driven culture: experimentation, analytics placement, decision rituals

### Takeaway
"Data-driven" is easy to claim and hard to fake in three places:
- the existence and scale of a self-serve experimentation platform and the methods it uses (variance reduction, geo-experiments);
- whether models and analytics sit inside core P&L decisions (credit, pricing, ranking) rather than in reporting;
- whether insiders can name decisions that analytics reversed against a senior opinion.

Both demo companies show strong evidence on the first two at company level. The third must be tested in interviews. Practitioner critics such as Benn Stancil warn that even "data-driven" executives mostly decide on directional judgment, so test the analyst's decision rights, not the slogans.

### Cited Findings
- **Nubank, official (2026-04-08):** the 20-F FY2025 cites "Proprietary Data Science" as a competitive advantage, "artificial intelligence and 60+ machine learning algorithms" across underwriting, personalization and support, and sixth-generation ML underwriting models in Mexico. It mentions an experimentation platform for testing models before live deployment and the acquisition of the US AI firm Hyperplane in July 2024. VERIFIED_OFFICIAL: [Nu 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691493/000129281426002166/nuform20f_2025.htm)
- **Nubank engineering blog:**
  - It says an experimentation culture helps "fight strong opinions (aka HiPPO)".
  - It describes a platform that automatically generates per-test metric datasets so decisions happen "without an analytics bottleneck", and a CUPED variance-reduction post drawing on years of tests across millions of customers and hundreds of metrics.
  - The People team A/B-tested an internal HR recognition product ("Noodles") and then paused it.
  - Post dates not verified (TO_VERIFY), and the posts are likely pre-2025. PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL (company-authored but technical): [Intro to A/B tests](https://building.nu.com/introduction-to-a-b-tests/); [A/B testing for HR products](https://building.nu.com/ab-testing-hr-products/); [Data & Analytics archive](https://building.nu.com/data/); [Scaling data analytics with SWE practices](https://building.nu.com/scaling-data-analytics-with-software-engineering-best-practices/)

- **Data-culture frameworks:**
  - A four-check framework: do leaders prioritize data (how often executives talk about and invest in it), can staff access and use it, are decisions actually evidence-based, and is the data trusted.
  - Interview guidance: ask who the analytics team reports to, how its structure changed in the last year, and what a peer sees as the biggest current problem. A vague answer, or one that conflicts with the hiring manager's, is a flag.
  - PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL: [Reichental, Medium](https://reichental.medium.com/four-questions-you-can-ask-to-evaluate-your-data-culture-ea5c7a598b54); [Learning Data, Medium](https://medium.com/learning-data/5-questions-to-ask-about-a-companys-data-culture-during-your-interview-6aca38f8dcc7)

### Inferences
- HYPOTHESIS: signals for a data-driven culture.
  - **Positive:** a self-serve experimentation platform with automated metric scorecards and variance reduction (Nu's CUPED, MELI's GeoLift). Experiments outside product as well, in HR and marketing. ML inside the P&L engine (credit, fraud, pricing, ranking). Analytics embedded in business units with a dotted or solid line to a central data org. Written decision docs with metric pre-registration. Weekly or monthly business reviews where the KPI owner, not the analyst, presents. Analysts named in decision records. Public technical writing with methods.
  - **Red flags:** analytics reporting to Finance or IT as a "reporting function". Dashboards counted as output. Experiments run but the "ship" decision overridden by seniority (HiPPO). No holdout or guardrail metrics. Analysts not invited to the decision meeting. "We'd like to be data-driven" language from a VP. Metric definitions owned by nobody.
- HYPOTHESIS: at Nubank and MELI, ML and experimentation are clearly core to credit and marketplace mechanics, so the company-level data-driven score is high. For an **analytics manager** role, though, the real risk is different: a mature platform plus heavy AI automation (MELI's "Verdi" decision agents, Nu's automated A/B datasets) can shrink the human analyst's decision rights to interpretation or reporting. Test in interviews whether the role owns decisions or serves them.
- HYPOTHESIS: Stancil's critique gives the interview test: "Name one significant decision in the last 12 months where analysis changed what a senior leader wanted to do, and who implemented it." Strong data cultures answer quickly and specifically.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 public figures for experiment volume (tests per year) at either company. Nubank's and MELI's engineering-blog experimentation posts appear to predate 2025, and their dates were not verified.
- I could not determine the reporting line of the central data or analytics organization (CDO vs CTO vs CFO) at either company from public 2025–2026 sources.
- No Locally Optimistic article specifically on evaluating an employer's data culture was found. Its community and blog are recommended in general, but I did not retrieve or cite a specific post.

## 5. Sources: where to find signals, reliability, biases, and how to separate employer branding from reality

### Takeaway
Rank evidence by how costly it is to fake:
1. how the company behaves in stress events;
2. audited or regulated disclosures;
3. specific accounts from current and former insiders;
4. verified-anonymous platforms (Blind);
5. unverified aggregates (Glassdoor, Indeed);
6. award rankings;
7. careers pages and branding.

Research shows Glassdoor tracks culture moderately at aggregate level but skews positive and is weak on specific facets. Awards measure participation and selected HR metrics rather than decision culture.

### Cited Findings
- **MIT SMR / Glassdoor Culture 500:** applied machine learning to about 1.2M Glassdoor reviews to score nine culture dimensions. MIT SMR warns that reviews may not be accurate if companies incentivize or pressure employees to write glowing ones, and notes that Glassdoor moderators reject about 10% of submitted reviews. PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL: [MIT SMR, Measuring Culture in Leading Companies](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/projects/measuring-culture-in-leading-companies/)
- **Validity study (US federal agencies; publication year about 2019, TO_VERIFY):** overall Glassdoor satisfaction correlated r = .516 with aggregated FEVS employee-survey results, but facet-level validity was "not well-supported". PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL: [Personnel Assessment and Decisions, Vol. 5](https://scholarworks.bgsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1099&context=pad)
- **Martirosyan & Kamdar (arXiv 2511.01086; v1 2025-11-02, v2 2026-05-03; accepted at AMCIS 2026):** over 300k ratings, coded with the Competing Values Framework and CultureBERT. Glassdoor (fully anonymous, unverified) skews positive and emphasizes clan and market culture. Blind (employment-verified, anonymous) emphasizes clan and hierarchy. Verification changes the portrayal but does not remove bias. PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL: [arXiv 2511.01086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01086)
- **Other academic work using Glassdoor text as a culture measure:** EPJ Data Science 2025 (organizational pride and psychological safety) and CHI 2020 (modeling organizational culture). PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL: [EPJ Data Science 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjds/s13688-025-00576-4); [CHI 2020](http://www.munmund.net/pubs/CHI20_Culture.pdf)

  - Mercado Livre: 4.1/5, 205 reviews, 78% would recommend.

  - [Glassdoor BR Nubank](https://static.glassdoor.com.br/Reviews/Nubank-Reviews-E827975_P2.htm); [Indeed BR Nubank](https://br.indeed.com/cmp/Nubank/reviews); [Glassdoor Mercado Livre](https://static.glassdoor.com.ar/Reviews/Mercado-Livre-Reviews-E2372230_P3.htm)

The two figures may describe different metrics, so the discrepancy needs checking. VERIFIED_OFFICIAL (10-K) vs TO_VERIFY (Impact Report summary): [MELI 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1099590/000109959026000006/meli-20251231.htm); [MELI 2025 Impact Report](https://sustentabilidadmercadolibre.com/en/blog/mercado-libre-presents-its-2025-impact-report)

### Inferences
- HYPOTHESIS: reliability and bias of each source (my synthesis; source-specific biases not individually verified are marked TO_VERIFY).

| Source | What it is good for | Main biases / limits | Reliability tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20-F / 10-K / proxy / 6-K / 8-K | Audited KPIs, headcount, equity breadth, governance, explicit trade-offs | Culture text is boilerplate; legally reviewed; no team-level view | High for numbers, low for culture claims |
| Shareholder and founder letters, earnings calls | How leaders reason about outcomes and trade-offs; analyst push-back | Selective framing; investor audience | Medium-high |
| Engineering and data blogs, conference talks | Methods (experimentation, ML), quality of thinking | Curated success stories; often old (check dates); written by tech, not business | Medium |
| Press on stress events (layoffs, RTO, disputes) | How values hold under pressure | Story-driven; one-sided unless both company and union/worker views are quoted | Medium-high when several outlets agree |
| Blind | Verified employees; candid on politics, levels, hierarchy | Skews to US bigtech and engineers; thin for Brazilian business roles; leans negative/hierarchy | Medium (low coverage in BR, TO_VERIFY) |
| Glassdoor (BR/global) | Aggregate trend over time; recurring themes in "cons" | Unverified, skews positive; incentive and pressure risk; weak facet validity; filter by date/role/location | Low-medium individually, medium in aggregate trend |
| Indeed BR | Frontline/ops roles | Small samples for corporate roles | Low-medium |
| Comparably | Rarely has LATAM samples | Sparse non-US data | Low (TO_VERIFY) |
| levels.fyi | Ladder structure, comp bands, vesting | Self-reported; engineering-heavy; small n at Director+ | Medium for structure, low-medium for exact numbers |
| LinkedIn (profiles, Talent Insights) | Tenure, talent flows, leadership backgrounds, functional growth | Self-reported profiles; lag; Premium needed for Insights | Medium-high for patterns |
| layoffs.fyi | Timeline of public cuts | Press-based; misses small/unreported cuts (e.g., MELI's 119 came only via Folha) | Medium for big cuts, low for completeness (TO_VERIFY methodology) |
| GPTW Brasil | Company opted in and ran a survey | Opt-in selection; certification is a paid service (TO_VERIFY); measures climate, not decision quality | Low-medium for this purpose |
| LinkedIn Top Companies BR | Career-progression signals at scale | Lagged data (calendar year before publication); measures mobility, not merit fairness | Medium for "growth", low for culture |
| Reclame Aqui | Customer-side execution quality (ops competence) | Customer complaints, not employee culture; volume scales with customer base | Low-medium, indirect |
| Backchannel (current and former employees, 2nd-degree) | Specific decisions, promotions, conflicts | Small n; personal grievances; network bias | Highest when ≥2 independent and specific |

- HYPOTHESIS: tests for separating employer branding from reality.
  1. **Specificity test.** Does the claim name a metric, a decision, a date and an owner? "We are data-driven" fails. "The Q3 pricing test reversed the CPO's proposal" passes.
  2. **Cost test.** Did living the value cost the company something visible, such as a killed product, a missed quarter explained honestly, or a reversed decision? MELI explicitly accepting a 17% YoY operating-income drop for investment is a costed statement.
  3. **Cross-channel consistency.** Compare investor letters, careers pages, Glassdoor "cons" from the last 12 months, Blind, and interview answers. Branding shows up only in the official channels; reality shows up in all of them.
  4. **Stress-event test.** Look at how cuts, return-to-office and disputes were handled: transparency of criteria, redeployment, whether dissent was handled through process.
  5. **Recency and scope.** Discard undated material. Downgrade anything older than 24 months. Weight team-level over company-level evidence.
  6. **Independence.** Two sources that both rely on the same press release count as one.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed reliability study specific to Glassdoor Brasil, Blind's Brazilian coverage, Comparably's LATAM coverage, or layoffs.fyi completeness.
- A study of Glassdoor's "give-to-get" policy, reportedly showing that modest incentives change review writing, appeared in search results without a confirmable URL, so it is excluded.
- GPTW Brasil's methodology and fee model were not fetched (TO_VERIFY).
- Reclame Aqui scores for Nubank and Mercado Livre (2025–2026) were not checked.

## 6. Scoring rubric and targeted questions (interviews, peers, backchannel)

### Takeaway
Score each criterion 0–3 against evidence thresholds, not impressions. A score of 2 or above requires at least one independent, non-company source. A score of 3 requires at least two specific insider accounts that agree with public artifacts and apply to the actual team being joined. For an analytics leader close to P&L, the decisive questions are about decision rights: which decisions changed because of analytics, who implemented them, and how disagreements with business leaders get resolved.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner sources recommend asking for concrete examples, comparing the hiring manager's answers with peers', asking about the analytics reporting line and recent team changes, and asking how feedback led to change. They also note that reporting directly to the CEO does not guarantee a voice or the freedom to drive change. PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL: [Yale CDO, 2025-09-26](https://cdo.som.yale.edu/blog/2025/09/26/how-to-evaluate-a-companys-culture-before-you-join/); [Learning Data, Medium](https://medium.com/learning-data/5-questions-to-ask-about-a-companys-data-culture-during-your-interview-6aca38f8dcc7); [Pipeline2Insights](https://pipeline2insights.substack.com/p/before-you-join-how-to-evaluate-a-company-culture-growth-politics)
- The interview is a two-way evaluation, and questions should be tailored to the interviewer's role. Keep HR screens for process questions, and save culture questions for managers and peers. Ask how you will be evaluated, what success looks like in the first months, and what the typical career paths are. PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL: [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/how-to-interview-the-company-that-is-interviewing-you-for-data-jobs-3b4fcce77c03); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/10-questions-data-scientists-should-ask-employers-during-a-job-interview/)
- Nubank's own blog names HiPPO as the failure mode its experimentation culture aims to counter. That gives a legitimate, non-confrontational way to ask how well it works in practice. PRACTICAL_EXTERNAL (company-authored): [Building Nubank, Intro to A/B tests](https://building.nu.com/introduction-to-a-b-tests/)

### Inferences
- HYPOTHESIS (method design): **scoring rubric, 0–3 per criterion.**

| Score | Evidence threshold (all criteria) |
|---|---|
| 0: Contradicted | At least two independent source types (e.g., recent reviews plus backchannel, or a stress event plus an evasive interview answer) contradict the claim; or interviewers cannot give any specific example |
| 1: Claimed | Only company-authored evidence (careers page, values, awards, blog) or generic interview answers; no independent confirmation |
| 2: Corroborated | Company evidence plus at least one independent source (audited disclosure with numbers, consistent multi-review pattern ≤24 months, or one specific insider account); at least one dated concrete example |
| 3: Demonstrated at team level | Score 2 plus at least two independent insiders (at least one former employee or non-reporting peer) giving specific, consistent examples ≤18 months old about the actual org being joined; behavior consistent through a stress event |

  Criterion-specific "3" anchors:
  - **Results:** the hiring manager names 2–3 outcome metrics the team moved, with baseline, target and actual; goals were reset when they were wrong; an ex-employee confirms that reviews cite impact evidence.
  - **Meritocracy:** the candidate sees the leveling doc or rubric, with calibration across managers. At least one recent promotion is explained by documented impact, and insiders confirm that disagreement upward is safe.
  - **Competence:** a sample of 20 or more director-level business and product profiles shows strong prior employers and internal promotions. Business partners in the loop demonstrate unit-economics fluency. Alumni land well.
  - **Data-driven:** at least two decisions in the last 12 months that analytics changed against a senior prior. An experiment platform with guardrails. Analytics present in decision forums. A clear metric owner for each KPI.

  Weighting for an analytics leader role: Data-driven 30%, Results 30%, Meritocracy 20%, Competence 20%. **Hard gates:** any criterion at 0 → stop or escalate checks. Data-driven ≤1 → the role is likely a reporting function. Company-level scores cap but do not set team-level scores.

  Confidence: record each score with an evidence count, separated by source type (official / independent / insider) and the date of the newest item.

- HYPOTHESIS (method design): **targeted questions.**
  - **Results orientation**
    - *Hiring manager:* "What two or three metrics will define success for this role in 12 months, and what are their current baselines?" "Tell me about a goal your team missed last half. What happened next?" "Which company-level KPI does this team own or move?"
    - *Peers:* "How are OKRs set here: top-down, negotiated, or copied from last year?" "What gets someone praised in a review: shipping, or impact?"
    - *Backchannel / former:* "When the team missed, was it a learning or a blame exercise?" "During the last cuts or reorganization, were decisions explained with outcomes?"
  - **Meritocracy**
    - *Hiring manager:* "Walk me through the last promotion on your team. What evidence was presented, and who calibrated it?" "How is the level for this role mapped against the ladder, and can I see the rubric?"
    - *Peers:* "Has someone been promoted without a strong sponsor?" "How visible are level and pay criteria?"
    - *Backchannel / former:* "Did politics or proximity to leaders matter more than results?" "What happens to people who disagree with a C-level decision in writing?" At Nubank, ask specifically how the November 2025 return-to-office dispute is perceived internally.
  - **Competence, business side**
    - *Hiring manager:* "Who are my main business counterparts? What is their background, and how long have they been in the role?" "What was the last decision a business partner made that you thought was excellent, and why?"
    - *Peers:* "Where do strong people leave to?" "Which business teams are known as strong or weak partners for analytics?"
    - *Backchannel / former:* "Would you work with this VP or Director again?" "Is the business side hired with a bar as high as engineering's?"
  - **Data-driven (analytics leader specific)**
    - *Hiring manager:*
      - "Name one decision in the last 12 months that analytics changed, and who implemented it."
      - "When analysis disagrees with a business leader, how is it resolved, and who has the final call?"
      - "Does analytics attend the forum where the decision is made, or receive the outcome afterwards?"
      - "What share of the team's time goes to recurring reporting versus decision support?"
      - "How is analytics performance measured: tickets closed, or decisions influenced and value attributed?"
      - "Who owns metric definitions?"
    - *Peers (analysts, PMs, business):* "When an A/B test result contradicts a senior leader's view, what usually happens?" "Can you show me a decision doc template?"
    - *Backchannel / former:* "Was analytics a partner or a dashboard factory?" "Did the analytics leader have budget, headcount influence, or a seat on the business unit's leadership team?"

### Gaps
- The weights and thresholds above are my design. I found no empirical validation of a culture-scoring rubric for job seekers.
- No source gave base rates (for example, what share of analytics teams at LATAM tech companies sit under Finance vs a CDO), so the "reporting function" gate is a judgment call.
